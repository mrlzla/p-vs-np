# P versus NP research directions

November 22, 2026

## The first direction to pursue

**Hardness magnification for the Minimum Circuit Size Problem (MCSP).** Given an $N=2^m$-bit truth table, decide whether the function it describes has a circuit of size at most $m^k$, for one fixed $k$. Let $G_k(m)$ be the minimum size of a fan-in-two De Morgan circuit that makes this decision on every truth table of length $N$. The CSLib Frontier development proves $P\ne NP$ from the following lower bound for any fixed $k$:

$$
\forall c>0\;\exists m\qquad G_k(m)> 2^m\,c\,(m+1)^c.
$$

This is a lower bound on the solver for the circuit minimization problem. The existential choice of $m$ can depend on $c$. In [MCSPTarget.lean](frontier/MCSPTarget.lean) the transfer reuses `Cslib.MCSP.P_ne_NP_of_yesSet_pow_hard`, and a second theorem checks that $G_k(m)\ge 2^m2^{\lfloor m/d\rfloor}$ infinitely often for any fixed positive integer $d$ is sufficient. The cutoff exponent $k$ and the lower-bound exponent $1/d$ are independent. The imported theorem uses the frontier library's multitape definition of $P$ and $NP$. [Pinned Lean source](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Computability/MCSP/Magnification.lean#L1008) · [McKay–Murray–Williams](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/MCSP-MKTP-stoc19.pdf)

## Checked MCSP lower bound

[MCSPRestrictions.lean](frontier/MCSPRestrictions.lean) uses the exact quadratic-cutoff slice of `GapMCSP.yesSet` and counts AND, OR, NOT and constant gates. For $m\ge 1$, let $b=\lfloor\log_2(m+1)\rfloor+1$ and $B(m)=4b+(3+4b)m^2$. The existing Boolean circuit counting lemma gives at most $2^{B(m)}$ accepted tables. In any truth-table restriction containing an accepted table, varying the inessential free positions produces an accepting subcube. Therefore the restriction has at most $B(m)$ such positions. With all $N=2^m$ positions free, the solver needs at least $N-B(m)$ essential inputs and thus at least $N-1-B(m)$ gates. The wire-accounting theorem adds a charge for each repeated live argument read and each missing argument slot relative to a binary gate. [Upstream MCSP semantics and counting](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Computability/MCSP/Basic.lean)

For the stronger bound, take certificates for the two accepted constant tables. In each certificate, at least $N-B(m)$ entries must be fixed because the remaining entries form an accepting subcube. Track each demand as a wire and a Boolean value. If the two certificates need the same wire value, they share that demand. If they need opposite values, their demands are distinct. A binary gate with a single demanded output value introduces at most two distinct argument demands. If both values are demanded, one value uses both arguments and the other uses one, for at most three demands. Backwards counting from the single required output gives a combined input demand of at most the number of gates plus one. The Lean theorem also charges repeated requests for the same argument state across different gates. The resulting unconditional inequality is

$$
G_2(m)\ge 2N-1-2B(m).
$$

For $m=16$, with $N=65{,}536$ and cutoff $256$, this gives $G_2(16)\ge119{,}255$. The bound is linear in $N$. The magnification target above still calls for growth beyond $N$ times every fixed power of $\log N$.

### Many accepted tables

[MCSPMultiCertificates.lean](frontier/MCSPMultiCertificates.lean) pools certificate requests over any finite sample containing the two constant tables. For each gate $k$, let $d_k$ count demanded output values and let $a_k$ count distinct requested argument wire/value pairs. Define $U=\sum_k(d_k+1-a_k)_+$ as the sum of unused allowances and $E=\sum_k(a_k-d_k-1)_+$ as the sum of excess charges. Let $R$ count repeated requests for the same state across gates after the first request. Every correct circuit $C$ satisfies

$$
2N+R+U\le |C|+1+2B(m)+E.
$$

Each excess charge is one precisely when the gate requests all four argument states. For an AND gate, the three input patterns $(0,1)$, $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$ suffice. The first two force different false argument states and the last requires both true states. A size bound above $2N$ follows from a sample with $R+U-E>1+2B(m)$. To obtain $(2+\varepsilon)N$, a margin of at least $1+2B(m)+\varepsilon N$ would suffice. These conditions would have to hold for every correct circuit, with the sample allowed to depend on the circuit.

[MCSPSampleControls.lean](frontier/MCSPSampleControls.lean) proves a limitation on arguments using a fixed number of accepting examples and acceptance-set sparsity. For any prescribed $t$ tables of length $N$, a circuit of at most $2N+5\cdot 2^t+t(3\cdot 2^t+2)+3$ gates accepts exactly that sample. It groups positions by their bit pattern across the samples, checks agreement within each group, and tests the resulting group values for membership in the sample. Thus even exact acceptance of a fixed-size sample is compatible with size $2N+O_t(1)$.

The two constant tables and the $m$ projection tables form a checked family of at most $m+2$ accepted probes with distinct signatures for all $N$ positions. The exact accounting identifies an additional obstruction to using these signatures with the existing charge. If $I$ is the number of distinct demanded input wire/value pairs, then

$$
R+U-E=|C|+1-I.
$$

Every demanded state is either the output demand or a requested argument state. The circuit's topological order keeps the output out of the argument set. Counting their disjoint union and balancing each gate's allowance gives the equality. On a fixed circuit, adding probes only enlarges the demanded input set. Therefore the projection pool's net charge is at most the two-corner pool's charge. On an MCSP solver it can drop by at most $2B(m)$, since the two corners already demand at least $2N-2B(m)$ of the $2N$ possible input states. Even if the projections demand all $2N$ states, proving a margin of $1+2B(m)+e$ is equivalent to proving $|C|\ge 2N+2B(m)+e$. This certificate count yields $2N-O(m^2\log m)$. The forest argument below supplies a stronger structural constraint. [Checked projection margin](frontier/MCSPMultiCertificates.lean)

### Communication cut

[MCSPCommunicationCut.lean](frontier/MCSPCommunicationCut.lean) embeds equality across a balanced partition of the truth-table positions for exact MCSP at cutoff $m^2$. Write a position as $(a,b,t)$ with an $r$-bit prefix $a$, split bit $b$, and $s$-bit suffix $t$, where $m=r+s+1$. An accepted probe is the repeated table of a prefix function $f(a)$. The input partition assigns $(a,b,t)$ to side $b\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}} h(t)$ for a selector $h$. Each side has $2^{r+s}$ positions.

For distinct prefix functions $f$ and $g$, the table formed by taking one side from $f$ and one from $g$ determines $h$ at a prefix on which the functions differ. The earlier bound of $2^{B(m)}$ accepted tables therefore bounds the number of selectors that incorrectly accept any fixed off-diagonal pair. A union bound over the $2^{2\cdot 2^r}$ ordered pairs finds a common selector whenever $2\cdot 2^r+B(m)<2^s$. The proof also bounds how many masks in the balanced family can fail. For $m=32$, the pinned Shannon synthesis constructs every 9-input prefix function with at most 848 gates, within the cutoff of 1024. The cut therefore embeds equality on 512 bits. [Pinned Shannon construction](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/ShannonConstruction.lean)

For **any placement of gates** consistent with such a partition of the inputs, at least 512 distinct source wires must feed a gate on the other side. If two equal-pair inputs have the same values on all crossing wires, induction along the circuit shows that a mixed input follows the corresponding original run on each side. Its output would then accept, contradicting the equality restriction. This works with crossings in either direction. The existing Lean fooling-set theorem also gives a 513-bit lower bound for deterministic protocols on the restricted problem. [Pinned fooling-set theorem](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Computability/Communication/FoolingSet.lean)

The cut bound constrains circuit wiring independently of the certificate count. Turning these crossings into a gate lower bound requires a separate charging argument. The forest proof below obtains a size bound from the two-corner structure and affine probes.

### Narrow-cut test

[MCSPNarrowCut.lean](frontier/MCSPNarrowCut.lean) tests whether a near-$2N$ gate budget, the equality restriction, and the acceptance-count bound together force a narrower cut. At arity 32, the control requires agreement on each 9-bit prefix block after omitting two suffixes. It accepts every prefix probe and rejects the splice of any two distinct probes across every mask in the verified family. A retained suffix gives a pair of opposite-side positions in each prefix block, so disagreement between the two probes violates that block's agreement check.

Each block uses a shared all-true or all-false test. With 512 blocks and $2^{22}-2$ retained suffixes, the verified size budget is $512[4(2^{22}-2)+5]+1=2N-1535$. One bit per block and the 2,048 free positions encode every accepted table, so the control meets the MCSP acceptance-count bound. The generalized crossing-wire theorem applies to every circuit computing the control, including a minimum circuit. Thus the size budget, equality probes and sparsity permit minimum circuits with no cut narrower than 512 in the verified family.

Exact MCSP accepts the split-bit projection, which the control rejects. Lean also proves that at most $B(m)$ split pairs can each be swapped on every table while preserving exact MCSP. Independent swaps of the accepted split-bit projection would otherwise produce more than $2^{B(m)}$ distinct accepted tables. A proof of the narrow-cut statement for exact MCSP needs to turn additional behavior such as this orientation constraint into a restriction on minimum-circuit wiring. The control addresses the available premises and leaves the exact-MCSP statement open.

### Orientation and wiring

[MCSPPairWiring.lean](frontier/MCSPPairWiring.lean) proves the wiring implication for every correct circuit, including minimum ones. If each gate either ignores both endpoints or reads exactly the two endpoints through AND or OR, exchanging the inputs leaves every internal gate value unchanged. The output of an MCSP circuit must be a gate because the all-zero table is accepted. The semantic swap bound then implies that at most $B(m)$ split pairs can have only these direct uses. Every other pair has a gate reading at least one endpoint in a way other than a paired AND or OR.

[MCSPSplitControl.lean](frontier/MCSPSplitControl.lean) checks a stronger control at arity 32. For each 9-bit prefix, the control enforces agreement separately for split bits zero and one. It omits four suffixes, leaving 4,096 entries free and another 1,024 bits to describe the constant rail values. It accepts every prefix probe and the split projection, meets the acceptance-count bound, and has a minimum circuit of size at most $2N-3071$. Swapping any retained pair in the accepted split projection violates agreement in its rail. Thus every circuit for the control has at most 2,048 symmetrically wired pairs, below the exact-MCSP allowance of 27,672.

Every good selector must vary among the retained suffixes. If it were constant there, four suffix minterms and one XOR with the split bit would give a circuit for the mask within the MCSP cutoff. Mixing the two constant probes would then accept, excluding that selector from the good family. When the selector varies, splicing distinct prefix probes produces disagreement within a rail. The existing equality restriction argument therefore forces at least 512 crossing wires for every good mask and every gate placement, including on the minimum control circuit. A suffix-coordinate projection supplies a checked table that exact MCSP accepts and this control rejects. The orientation wiring constraint and the previously checked cut data still leave the narrow-cut claim for minimum exact-MCSP circuits open.

J. A. Wittcop's November preprint reports a Lean-checked $(2-o(1))N$ bound at cutoff $m-1$ using restricted truth tables and gate elimination. Its cutoff and full binary basis provide a calibration point. The exact $m^2$ cutoff needs its own structural analysis. Test any candidate invariant against small arbitrary-function gates as well, since the locality barrier explains how apparently strong restriction arguments can still run into efficient circuits equipped with such gates. [Wittcop preprint](https://www.academia.edu/180415409/2N_Circuit_Lower_Bounds_for_the_Minimum_Circuit_Size_Problem) · [Locality barrier](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.70)

## Brilliant P versus NP audit

On November 23, 2026, the public project described P versus NP as open. It publishes attempted proof steps and their status alongside a Lean repository. Its meta-complexity program is investigating short, fixed-format local reductions from parity to total MCSP. Its general-circuit program is investigating an `n^1.01` lower bound for SAT. A bound of that order for SAT would rule out one circuit size regime, while the Cook–Levin target needs to rule out every polynomial running time. The SAT algorithm target and parity reduction obstruction remain open in the project record. [Project and programs](https://pequalnp.com/programs) · [Parity target](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-056) · [SAT circuit target](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-clb-036) · [SAT algorithm target](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-alg-001)

The project's highlighted November 23 update reports exact gate counts for specified families of graph edge functions. The function asks whether any edge has both endpoints set to true. In the stated families the count is $|E|+\tau(G)-1$, where $\tau(G)$ is the minimum vertex-cover size. These give concrete controls for gate elimination and sharing. Using a graph bound in the exact MCSP argument would first require a restriction of the MCSP decider that realizes one of the covered graph functions. [Exact graph circuit counts](https://pequalnp.com/article#A-032)

### Restriction savings and shared wiring

[RestrictionWiring.lean](pequalnp/RestrictionWiring.lean) combines four upstream results about minimum circuits. For an essential input whose two restrictions are nonconstant, one setting saves at least two gates. If neither did, the same reader would have to be an AND gate from the true restriction and an OR gate from the false restriction. Separately, a wire whose true value implies acceptance on every input can have at most one gate reader in a minimum circuit. This bounds direct fan-out once a semantic implication has been proved. [False restriction](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-169) · [Unique reader](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-170) · [True restriction](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-171) · [Wire absorption](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-193)

The first application is to prove a lower bound for one-bit restrictions of the exact MCSP decider, then test whether that bound reaches within one gate of a candidate minimum at enough positions to constrain a cut. The direct wiring statement needs that numerical hypothesis in addition to essentiality. The absorption result offers a separate test for wires that certify acceptance by themselves. Both tests should first be applied to the existing split control to see which hypotheses distinguish exact MCSP.

The releases and circuit semantics differ. Brilliant uses Lean 4.36.0 and circuits encoded as lists. Out-of-range wires read false for free. The CSLib Frontier snapshot uses 4.36.0-rc2, typed preceding wires, and counts a constant gate. For an exact gate-count or minimum-circuit transfer, prove a gate-count and reader-preserving translation from CSLib and a size-nonincreasing return translation for nonconstant functions. The return translation needs to normalize defaults and short gate inputs. Also match truth-table indexing before transporting a statement about a specific membership bit. [Pinned circuit semantics](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/218e3a7546f367bbe34ca460aeec74dcec949a69/lean/PEqualNP/Defs/CircuitLowerBounds.lean)

### The parity-grid proposal at the quadratic cutoff

[GridCriterion.lean](pequalnp/GridCriterion.lean) checks that the numerical hypothesis of `L-meta-060` fails for every power-of-two grid with at least two columns when the represented function has arity `m ≥ 32` and the cutoff is `m²`. For $R$ rows and $N$ total bits there are at least $2^{N-R}$ row-even tables. The upstream easy-table count implies that at least half of them are hard at this cutoff. Multiplying this lower bound by the criterion's factors already reaches $2^N$, precluding its strict inequality. The result concerns this sufficient criterion at this cutoff. Other local reductions and cutoffs remain questions. [Grid criterion](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-060) · [Upstream table-counting lemma](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/218e3a7546f367bbe34ca460aeec74dcec949a69/lean/PEqualNP/MetaComplexity/Helpers/Natural.lean#L127)

[L_meta_060.lean](pequalnp/proposals/L_meta_060.lean) proves the project's conditional construction itself. Start from an arbitrary grid and toggle its odd rows by the column mask obtained by flipping the first bit of the parity input. An odd input gives a mask of even parity, so the grid transformation preserves row parities and is invertible. An even input gives a mask of odd parity, so the result has even rows. The transformed grid together with the original row parities still determines the original grid. The resulting bounds on the two classes of bad seeds give the posted union bound. The size header is fixed and each output table entry depends on at most its corresponding input column. Brilliant's checker accepted the exact posted statement in the isolated server checkout. The proof remains a local contribution draft. [Posted grid theorem](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-060)

[L_meta_066.lean](pequalnp/proposals/L_meta_066.lean) proves `PEqualNP.Outside.L_meta_066` in the same upstream circuit model: if $f : (\operatorname{Fin} n \to \operatorname{Bool}) \to \operatorname{Bool}$ has a fan-in-two circuit of size at most $s$ and $u : \operatorname{Fin} n \to \operatorname{Bool}$ selects a nonempty coordinate subset $S = \{i \mid u(i) = \text{true}\}$, then $x \mapsto f(x) \lor \bigwedge_{i \in S} x_i$ has a fan-in-two circuit of size at most $s + |S|$ (`append_and_chain` threads $|S|-1$ AND gates from the first selected input wire and joins the resulting conjunction with the existing output wire using one OR gate).

### One-bit restrictions and the split control

[MCSPPinning.lean](frontier/MCSPPinning.lean) gives a lower bound for **every** one-bit restriction of exact MCSP at cutoff $m^2$, for $m\ge3$. The $N-1$ remaining bits have two opposite accepted assignments. The first lifts to the constant table matching the pinned bit. The other lifts to the complement of that constant with an exception at the pinned entry, which takes at most $2m+2$ gates. Each of the two certificates fixes at least $N-1-B(m)$ bits by sparsity, and their claimed input states are disjoint. Thus each restricted decider requires at least $2(N-1)-1-2B(m)$ gates. For each position, the branch with fewer accepted tables has at most half the global count. Using $B(m)-1$ for that branch improves its bound by two gates to $L=2N-1-2B(m)$. At $m=32$ these bounds are $2N-55{,}347$ and $2N-55{,}345$, respectively. The proof also gives nonconstant restrictions whenever $B(m)<N-1$.

[MCSPSplitPinning.lean](frontier/MCSPSplitPinning.lean) gives a quantitative comparison on the existing split control. With $D$ retained inputs, both constant accepted tables require certificates that fix all $D$ entries, since each single retained-bit flip violates agreement. Thus the full control requires at least $2D-1$ gates. Fixing either value of a retained input makes its rail a single conjunction of the remaining literals. The checked upper bound for the restriction is at least $4{,}189{,}181$ gates below the lower bound for the full control. Omitted inputs are inessential.

[PinningWiring.lean](pequalnp/PinningWiring.lean) combines Brilliant's unique-reader and gate-type lemmas in its own circuit model. A nonconstant restriction with a size lower bound $L$ forces a single AND reader for a true pin or OR reader for a false pin when the input is essential and the minimum full circuit has at most $L+1$ gates. Using the smaller exact-MCSP branch would still require the gate-count-preserving model translation and a full minimum within that one-gate window. The split control's measured gap misses this premise at every retained input. The same file uses Brilliant's duplication lemmas to show that a minimum circuit can have a forced AND or OR reader while retaining an equality subproblem of the same width. The extra input is fixed to the neutral value in every probe, so any sound cut signature must still distinguish every probe. This tests local forcing in isolation. A narrower cut for exact MCSP would require a constraint on how readers across many split pairs connect downstream. [False pin](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-168) · [True pin](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-170) · [AND duplication](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-175) · [OR duplication](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-176)

### Downstream connections and shared readers

[MCSPDownstream.lean](frontier/MCSPDownstream.lean) gives an exact test for a proposed set of crossing source wires. Delete every outgoing connection from each proposed source and view the remaining edges as undirected. A placement with crossings contained in the proposed set exists exactly when no component contains inputs from opposite sides. In particular, two surviving reader paths into the same downstream gate block the proposed placement. Putting the common gate's output in the proposed set leaves the two incoming paths connected.

For every good mask at arity 32, Lean matches each of the 512 independent prefix coordinates of exact MCSP to a distinct crossing source in its downstream cone. Varying any subset of prefix coordinates gives a smaller equality restriction. Crossing wires outside those cones have fixed traces on the smaller restriction, so the union of the cones must supply at least as many crossing sources as the size of the subset. Hall's theorem gives the matching. The matched sources are distinct. Their incoming paths may overlap.

[MCSPRefinedCut.lean](frontier/MCSPRefinedCut.lean) raises the exact bound to 544 on a mask from the same family. Choose 32 prefixes by fixing four of the nine prefix bits and allow the two rails to take independent values there. A base nine-bit function uses at most 848 gates. A five-bit correction on the selected prefixes, enabled on one rail, brings the total to at most 991 gates. All 544 independent probe bits therefore fit the $1{,}024$-gate cutoff. For any different pair of probe assignments, the mixed table recovers the selector from a rail where they differ. Counting the accepted mixed tables and avoiding the original bad masks gives a selector satisfying both equality restrictions. The downstream matching applies to the new restriction.

On the split control, the two rails of each prefix can be assigned independently. Every good mask varies among retained suffixes, so mixing two different rail assignments rejects in a rail where they disagree. The same argument gives 1,024 distinct crossing sources, one per rail, for every gate placement on every good mask. This includes a minimum circuit of size at most $2N-3071$. Thus the near-$2N$ size budget and the previously verified split-pair wiring behavior also coexist with this stronger downstream obstruction.

Brilliant's absorption lemma applies to a wire whose truth alone certifies acceptance. [RestrictionWiring.lean](pequalnp/RestrictionWiring.lean) combines it with the sparsity count. Such a wire, if true on some input, needs a supporting set of at least $N-B$ inputs when the decider accepts at most $2^B$ tables. In a minimum circuit it has at most one effective gate reader. The supporting-set argument is also checked in CSLib and forces that wire's cone to touch both sides of every balanced split mask for exact MCSP and the split control. The reader bound was checked in Brilliant's circuit model. Ordinary shared gates may fail to certify acceptance on their own. [Brilliant's absorption lemma](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-193)

Both frontier files passed on the research server with Lean 4.36.0-rc2. The absorption extension passed in Brilliant's pinned Lean 4.36.0 checkout. All three used warnings as errors and the axiom audit reported only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. The exact MCSP gate-count lower bound remains near $2N$. Turning the cut obstruction into a larger gate bound requires a charge to the circuit size that cannot be met by the split control.

### Charging repeated requests on the matched wires

[MCSPMatchedSignals.lean](frontier/MCSPMatchedSignals.lean) strengthens the matching by requiring the value on a matched crossing wire to change when only its assigned probe coordinate changes. For a subset of coordinates, freeze every other coordinate, then apply the splice argument to the wires that can change. Hall's condition again gives distinct representatives. Give each gate to the true side if any of its supporting inputs lies there. Every crossing source for that placement depends only on the false side. Both values of each matched signal occur on accepted diagonal probes. Mixing the unchanged false half with a different true half shows that both values also occur on rejected tables. Lean also checks that assigning all gates to the true side supplies a 544-wire matching made entirely of input wires. A matching of signals alone supplies neither internal gates nor repeated gate readers.

[MCSPMatchedCharge.lean](frontier/MCSPMatchedCharge.lean) counts actual repeats. For a demanded wire/value pair with $d$ different certificate readers, the gross charge is $d-1$. Summing over all demanded states gives exactly the earlier repeat term. Restricting this sum to matched sources bounds its contribution to the old net margin, after unused allowances and excess demands are included. At $m=32$, the existing corner count requires net charge at least $55{,}346$ to prove more than $2N$ gates. A single unit for each polarity of each of the 544 wires gives at most $1{,}088$ units. The cut matching does not give the required multiplicities or a bound on the excess demands.

[MCSPCornerCube.lean](frontier/MCSPCornerCube.lean) checks that each constant table is in an accepting subcube with 544 free entries. Fix the 22 suffix bits of the represented input. Vary all 512 prefix entries on the zero rail and 32 entries on the one rail. The two guarded Shannon constructions fit the cutoff with at most 1,021 gates. Thus a uniform lower bound on the size of arbitrary semantic corner certificates is at most $N-544$. Even an optimal bound of that kind, combined with up to one unit for each polarity of each matched wire, contributes at most $2(N-544)+1088=2N$ before the final one-gate subtraction. A stronger argument could use the structure of the certificates generated by circuits or force multiple repeat charges per state.

The split control realizes the same refined probes on the same verified mask. Its larger rail probe family also has 1,024 distinct matched signals with the same accept/reject property for both values. Every pooled sample containing the two constants demands both values at every retained entry, for at least $2(N-4096)$ input states. On a minimum circuit of size at most $2N-3071$, the checked net identity gives at most $5{,}122$ net units, even when counting all repeated requests. This bounds the tested charge for every choice of matched sources in that circuit.

[RestrictionWiring.lean](pequalnp/RestrictionWiring.lean) reuses Brilliant's absorption rewrite inside a replaceable block. A wire whose true value forces the block's output can have at most one effective reader within that block in a minimum full circuit, provided the block can be replaced at its input boundary with the stated gate budget. The premise involves the local output, so the wire can fail to certify global acceptance. The matching supplies no such replaceable block for exact MCSP. [Brilliant's absorption lemma](https://pequalnp.com/lemma#L-meta-193)

The frontier proofs were replayed on the research server in the pinned 4.36.0-rc2 checkout, and the absorption extension in Brilliant's pinned 4.36.0 checkout. The files build with warnings as errors. The charge tested here falls short of the threshold, while the forest argument below proves the strict bound.

### Transporting cuts by permuting represented variables

[MCSPPermutedCut.lean](frontier/MCSPPermutedCut.lean) proves that permuting the variables of the represented function preserves exact quadratic MCSP. The wiring circuit for a coordinate permutation costs zero gates, and the inverse permutation gives equality of gate counts. On one exact-MCSP decision circuit, the 544-coordinate matching therefore transfers to the original cut and to each of the 22 split–suffix swaps, for every compatible gate placement. Assigning all gates to one side yields input-only matchings on all these cuts.

The reader count uses the union of matched source wires and one pool of certificates. Each wire/value pair contributes once, with multiplicity equal to its number of distinct requesting gates minus one. The net margin of the full pool equals the original margin minus the number of new demanded input states. Thus pooling these probes can reveal repeated readers, but the existing net certificate accounting cannot gain from pooling alone.

[MCSPInvariantControl.lean](frontier/MCSPInvariantControl.lean) and [MCSPOrbitCharge.lean](frontier/MCSPOrbitCharge.lean) give a control invariant under every permutation of the 23 split and suffix coordinates. The nine prefix coordinates are fixed. For each prefix, it omits the 23 tail positions of Hamming weight one and requires agreement independently in the two bands of weight at most three and weight greater than three. ### Scope of the Frontier ledger and the remaining growth target

The forest's output factorization is a useful interface for a circuit contraction. To apply `Ledger.ofAggregates` one would need to give the contracted program and prove the exact aggregate semantics of each special gate, including its boundary wires. The common-witness lemmas suffice for the current perturbation proof. The forest result gives $G_2(m)\ge(2+1/2280)N-O(m^2\log m)$, which is linear in truth-table length.

The existing Frontier ledger theorem assumes a dense, quantitatively rectangle-free accepted set. Exact MCSP's yes-set is sparse. Its complement is dense and contains large rectangles. [MCSPFrontierScope.lean](frontier/MCSPFrontierScope.lean) proves the latter by removing all left-hand projections of accepted tables and leaving the entire right side available. On a balanced split each side of the resulting rejecting rectangle has size at least $2^{N/2}-2^{B(m)}$. The existing dense rectangle-free theorem therefore needs a new application or a different hypothesis for this MCSP route. The Gaussian layout gives a constant circuit coefficient even when its hypotheses apply. [Pinned ledger theorem](https://github.com/SamuelSchlesinger/complexitylib/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Frontier/Ledger/LowerBound.lean) · [Pinned layout theorem](https://github.com/SamuelSchlesinger/complexitylib/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Frontier/Layouts/Gaussian.lean)

An intermediate growth target is $G_2(m)=\Omega(N\log N)$. A first test is to prove a two-scale restriction lemma that charges distinct gates at each scale, with repeated charges explicitly accounted for. The current one-scale theorem charges $\Omega(N)$ once and the ledger width bound grows with total slack. A proof using the same route needs a mechanism for charging many scales, or another structural theorem that remains informative at larger circuit sizes. The sufficient P versus NP target in [MCSPTarget.lean](frontier/MCSPTarget.lean) is to beat $cN(m+1)^c$ for every positive $c$ for one fixed cutoff exponent. An infinitely-often $N^{1+\varepsilon}$ bound for any fixed $\varepsilon>0$ would suffice.

### Two nested faces and disjoint gate charges

[MCSPNestedFaces.lean](frontier/MCSPNestedFaces.lean) fixes the represented inputs' leading bits to zero one at a time by setting the corresponding off-face truth-table entries to false. If the outer table has length $N=2^{m+2}$, the middle and inner tables have lengths $N/2$ and $N/4$. Each zero-fill embedding is injective, so both restricted decision functions inherit the outer acceptance-count bound $2^{B(m+2)}$. Extending an affine function off its face by zero costs at most two gates per step. For $m\ge5$, both faces therefore satisfy the affine-probe hypothesis of the existing forest lower bound at the outer quadratic cutoff.

[MCSPRestrictionCharges.lean](frontier/MCSPRestrictionCharges.lean) first partitions the original gate indices by the first stage at which a wire becomes constant. [MCSPFreshCharges.lean](frontier/MCSPFreshCharges.lean) strengthens the partition by removing gates whose restricted functions are constants, input projections, or equal to the restricted function of an earlier gate. Each such equality persists at subsequent restrictions. The reconstruction processes the original gate indices in order and uses at most one new gate for each index still needed, plus at most two shared constants.

For each newly pinned essential input, take the earliest gate that depends on it and computes a function different from its projection. That gate has an argument equal to the projection before pinning. After pinning, a buffer-free AND, OR, or NOT with that constant input produces a constant or an already available argument. A gate can be charged by at most two pinned inputs because each one needs a different argument pin. Sparsity bounds the number of inessential pinned slots by $B(m+2)$. [MCSPTwoScaleFresh.lean](frontier/MCSPTwoScaleFresh.lean) checks the disjoint charges

$$
|D_1|\ge \frac{N/2-B(m+2)}{2},\qquad
|D_2|\ge \frac{N/4-B(m+2)}{2},
$$

where $D_2$ consists solely of original indices that survive the first restriction and can be omitted after the second. At outer arity 32, the checked second-stage guarantee is 536,857,076 original gates. Adding both charged sets to the inner forest bound is valid. The guaranteed deletions recover less than the drop in the lower bound from $N$ to $N/4$, so this step leaves the strongest outer gate-count lower bound unchanged. All four files passed on the research server with warnings as errors.

### Adaptive pinning

[MCSPAdaptivePinning.lean](frontier/MCSPAdaptivePinning.lean) chooses a still-essential target entry, pins it to false, and rechecks which of the remaining entries are essential. Each singleton step makes at least one previously nonredundant gate redundant. Redundancy persists, so each pin charges a distinct original gate index. If no essential target remains, the all-zero table and every variation on the remaining targets are accepted. Sparsity therefore leaves at most $B(m+2)$ uncharged entries at each stage.

The stronger disjoint guarantees are $|D_1|\ge N/2-B(m+2)$ and $|D_2|\ge N/4-B(m+2)$. At outer arity 32 the second stage alone removes at least 1,073,714,152 previously nonredundant indices. With $M=N/4$, the inner forest bound and both charges give a full-circuit lower bound of $5M+\lfloor M/2280\rfloor-5B(m+2)-3$, or 5,369,041,696 gates at outer arity 32. Lean checks that the direct outer forest bound dominates this expression at every arity where both apply. At arity 32 the direct bound is 8,591,735,332 gates. The adaptive pinning proof and numerical comparison passed on the research server with warnings as errors.

### Adaptive pinning on the full table

[MCSPForestPinning.lean](frontier/MCSPForestPinning.lean) fixes the forest mask $P$ of an accepted affine table, then selects an inclusion-minimal extension $Q$ among the raw inputs read by stop gates. Every entry of $Q$ stays essential until pinned. The fresh-gate argument gives $|Q|-1$ distinct original gate losses during this second stage. The final pin can reach an output projection directly, accounting for the one allowed exception.

Stop gates are disjoint from the clean OR and AND forests. Every raw input in $Q$ has a reader among the stop gates that are redundant by the end of pinning. If $A$ of these stop gates were already redundant after the forest mask and $D$ become redundant during the stop-input pins, then $|Q|\le 2(A+D)$. The two sets are disjoint. The factor of two is the stop gates' fan-in. A guarantee on $D$ alone would require a further bound on $A$.

The full-size improvement uses the stop gates' fan-in once for both raw input wires and forest-root wires. With $R$ forest roots, $C$ raw stop inputs, $H$ stop gates, and $L$ two-corner slack, [MCSPMonotoneForest.lean](frontier/MCSPMonotoneForest.lean) gives $C+R\le 10B+1326L+2H$. [MCSPSuperTwoN.lean](frontier/MCSPSuperTwoN.lean) charges only the extra leaf pins beyond the first in its affine average and uses the disjoint leaf sets within each forest. It gives $12|P|\le 13R+2N$. Combining $N\le |P|+|Q|+B$, $|Q|\le C$, and $H\le58L$ yields $10N\le142B+18746L$. This strengthens the full-size lower bound through the two-corner slack identity. At outer arity 32 the revised bound is 8,592,170,176 gates, or 434,844 above the prior reported bound.

### Matching certified pairs to original stop gates

[MCSPStopMatching.lean](frontier/MCSPStopMatching.lean) takes a set of free targets $J$ and assigns each one a free anchor outside $J$, from a set $T$. The wiring hypothesis supplies a fresh original AND or OR stop gate that directly reads each anchor-target pair. Fan-in two forces those joint readers to be distinct. For each target certified by the two-bit test, the absorbing-branch argument supplies a bypass. A bypass lies outside all the joint readers and can read at most two targets. Keeping one representative per bypass therefore matches at least half of the certified targets to distinct additional original indices.

The exact MCSP separation theorem discards at most $2B|T|$ targets. Let $D$ be the number of fresh stop gates that become redundant when pinning $J$ to the original affine table, and $A$ the number of already-redundant, nonconstant stop gates reading some target in $J$. The checked bounds are
\[
D \ge |J| + \max\!\left(0,\left\lceil{\frac{(|J|-2B|T|)_+}{2}}\right\rceil-A\right),
\qquad
3|J| \le 2|\mathrm{StopGate}| + 2B|T| .
\]
The anchor restrictions are used to find the bypasses. The charged gate indices are lost when pinning the targets at the original forest restriction, and remain lost when the target pins are included in a larger adaptive pin set.

The checked arithmetic example is compatible with the previous forest budgets, a linear target family, and one early alias for every matched bypass. In it $D=|J|$. A separate finite example places every edge of a degree-one candidate graph inside the exception sets even when each exception set has size one. These examples delimit the counting argument. They leave open which configurations occur in minimum exact-MCSP circuits. The full-size numerical lower bound remains 8,592,176,548 gates. ### Rank-1 quadratic probes break the affine 2-plane bottleneck

On any $d$-dimensional affine subspace with $|U| = 4$ (or more generally $|U| = \frac{3}{4} \cdot 2^d$), affine functions suffer from the linear 2-plane obstruction ($x \oplus y \oplus z$ fixes a fourth value, forcing $E(4) = 3/8$ for degree-1 averaging and forcing the $+ 2n$ linear penalty in `exists_weighted_affine_forest_mask_witness`). \]
requiring complexity at most $12m + 8 \le m^2$ for all $m \ge 13$ (`complexity_quad_le`, `mcspSquare_accepts_quad`, `patched_quad_accepted`):

- **Exact $\mathbb{F}_2$ Fourier orthogonality & quadratic 2-plane vanishing (`card_quad_const_mul_le`):** Over any subset $U \subseteq \{0,1\}^m$, self-contained $\mathbb{F}_2$ span/orthogonality counting (`card_orth_mul_card_span`, `card_evalDots_fiber`, `card_constAffines_false`, `card_constQuad_eq`) pairs with the discrete derivative identity (`det_zero_of_quad_const_on_square`: any 2-plane square $\{y, y\oplus u_1, y\oplus u_2, y\oplus u_1\oplus u_2\} \subseteq U$ on which `quad` is constant forces $(c \cdot u_1)(d \cdot u_2) \oplus (c \cdot u_2)(d \cdot u_1) = 0$, satisfied by $10/16 = 5/8$ of $(c,d)$ pairs for one square via `card_det_zero_pair` and at most $3 \cdot 2^d$ pairs over a whole basis via `card_det_zero_basis`). The gain in downstream losses grows with the represented arity $m$, while the truth-table length is $N=2^m$.

The November 25 public feed announced a checked 102-gate lower bound for the monotone edge function of a specific 45-by-45 bipartite cycle instance. It also reported finite one-NOT elimination results for several seven-vertex graphs. The project still lists P versus NP as open. [Public project status](https://pequalnp.com/) · [Merged cycle proof](https://github.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/pull/14188)

### Following a matched exit to a second downstream loss

[MCSPSecondBlockExits.lean](frontier/MCSPSecondBlockExits.lean) extends the block-tag list to raw inputs at distance up to two predecessor gates. Each list has at most 18 tags. The same greedy selection works with these wider lists and still permits three fully covered blocks at arity 32 in the relevant circuit-size range.

A constant matched exit has a later downstream reader. If its next reader also directly reads a pin, the two inputs are fixed and the constant can continue through that gate. A nonconstant residual output forces the path to leave the direct readers. The wider tag lists give an injection from all first exits and one second exit per constant first exit into the original downstream reader indices. The second exits are strictly later than their own first exits. The full residual forest inequality therefore adds one gate per constant matched exit, with a bound of $8{,}620{,}564{,}205+C$ for the three-block arity-32 certificate when $C$ first exits become constant.

The minimum four-input control $((p\lor u)\land v)\lor w$ shows why another hypothesis is needed. Pinning the separated block containing $p$ to one makes the first exit equal to the surviving input $v$. The next gate still computes $v\lor w$. Lean verifies that for either pin value at most one original index outside the direct-reader set is omitted from the surviving circuit, counting both aliases and dead gates. For exact MCSP, the open step is to force many matched exits to be constant or to show that their aliases themselves force further deletions. The second-layer argument supplies a conditional improvement to the joint-pinning bound.

### Choosing the controlling value at a matched first exit

[MCSPConstantBlockExits.lean](frontier/MCSPConstantBlockExits.lean) covers a new wiring condition. If a block contains both original raw inputs of a binary gate, uniform pins can make that gate zero or one at will. We locate a route through the direct readers before assigning the pins. Every reader along that route has one fixed predecessor and one pinned raw argument of the same value, so the value propagates to the first exit. Choosing the uniform value to be the exit's absorbing value makes the exit itself constant. Other blocks keep the earlier one-exit matching. The extended footprint condition and second-layer matching charge one additional original gate per block with such a raw pair. A minimum four-input control verifies a case with two downstream deletions.

The greedy selector now accepts an explicit pool of eligible prefixes. For a pool of covered raw-pair blocks, a footprint capacity of $18g<K(H+1)$ and $r(18K+1)+H$ eligible prefixes suffices to choose $r$ separated blocks. If their joint repairs fit the cutoff and the padded count, the checked exact-MCSP lower bound adds $2r$ downstream gates to the direct-reader and forest terms. A growing lower bound on the size of such a pool in minimum exact-MCSP circuits remains open.

Retaining all dyadic ranges in the description-count factorial gives $1024!\ge2^{8204}$. The unpinned quadratic forest then proves **8,620,572,811 gates** at arity 32. With 768 pins the same estimate gives a residual description budget of 20,236 bits. The three-block joint bound is **8,620,571,616 plus the number of raw-pair blocks**. The numerical improvement in the unconditional bound comes from the tighter counting estimate.

### How many raw-pair blocks does the original circuit supply?

[MCSPRawPairPool.lean](frontier/MCSPRawPairPool.lean) gives a gate-count criterion for the missing supply. In a minimum circuit, the operation and its two original raw addresses determine the original gate trace and hence its index. A block of size $L$ therefore contains at most $2L^2$ distinct gates with both raw arguments in that block. If $A$ is the total number of such gates and $P$ is the number of covered qualifying prefixes, Lean proves $A\le 2L^2(P+B)$ for the zero-corner certificate. At most $B$ prefixes lose coverage. With enough such gates to make $P\ge r(18K+1)+H$ and $18g<K(H+1)$, the checked theorem selects $r$ separated blocks and invokes the second-exit matching, subject to the existing repair and sparsity budgets.

The new minimum control family computes the all-equal predicate with interleaved OR and AND chains and $2N$ gates. It accepts exactly the two constant tables. Every input belongs to both corner certificates in any implementation of this predicate, and the two chains' only raw pair is the same two addresses. Thus the minimum controls have at most one qualifying block for any prefix split. For a nonempty standard prefix, their two raw addresses even have different tags, so the covered pool is empty. This rules out deducing raw-pair abundance from minimum size, sparsity and two-corner coverage alone. The control rejects projection tables that exact MCSP accepts, leaving open whether further exact-MCSP probes force the required wiring statistic.

### What the probes force about raw readers

[MCSPProbePairBarrier.lean](frontier/MCSPProbePairBarrier.lean) tests summaries that retain the AND and OR of a block, plus the original bits outside it. A suffix projection and a rank-one product of two suffix bits each give an accepted table that is mixed in every block. Fixing one zero and one one per summarized block leaves every other bit of those blocks free while preserving the flags. Sparsity therefore allows at most $B/(2^d-2)$ blocks with such a summary, even when the summary property is supplied individually for each block. At arity 32, at least **16,777,108** of the 256-address blocks require distinctions beyond the two flags.

The same file verifies a degree-two checker of size at most $11N+m^2$ that accepts all projection and rank-one quadratic probes and meets the original sparsity bound. It reads raw inputs only through unary NOT gates. The circuit is an explicit construction. For full exact MCSP, composing any solver with the truth-table duality also gives a circuit with this raw-reader property at an overhead of $N$ gates.

[MCSPRawReaderNecessity.lean](frontier/MCSPRawReaderNecessity.lean) uses minimum size to prove a positive requirement in exact MCSP. In the complemented-input computation each direct raw NOT becomes a free projection. A negation only has to be supplied for an address read directly by a binary gate. Truth-table duality makes this complemented-input problem have the same circuit complexity as exact MCSP. Thus the number of direct raw NOTs in a minimum circuit is at most the number of distinct binary-read addresses. Combining this with the essential-input count forces at least $(N-B)/2$ distinct raw addresses to feed binary gates, or **2,147,473,914** addresses at arity 32 using the tighter factorial count. The missing structural step is a lower bound on how often two such addresses meet at the same original gate within one covered block. That is the statistic required by the second-exit theorem.

### Charge either a second exit or the unpaired direct readers

[MCSPBlockChargeDichotomy.lean](frontier/MCSPBlockChargeDichotomy.lean) combines binary-read necessity with the block-flag obstruction when choosing candidate prefixes. A candidate meets at least one binary-read raw address, is fully covered by the zero-corner certificate, and admits distinctions beyond its AND and OR. The candidate count suffices for the existing greedy selection with extended block tags.

For a selected block of $L=2^d$ addresses, a raw-pair gate supplies the controlling choice for a second exit. Pair-free blocks can instead charge one distinct original direct reader per address, because a collision would itself be a raw pair. Separation prevents collisions across blocks. If $u$ of the $r$ selected blocks are pair-free, the joint pin charges at least $r(L/2+2)+u(L/2-1)$ original gates outside the surviving fresh set. First and second exits lie outside all direct readers. The residual keeps the earlier quadratic forest bound.

On the growing schedule $m=2^{2t}$ with $t\ge8$, the paired/unpaired bound adds an unconditional $m/4$ gates to the previous separated-block charge. At arity 32 it gives 390 charged gates from the three blocks and a bound of **8,620,571,619 gates** for the joint argument. The independent unpinned factorial count still gives the stronger finite bound at arity 32. The proof combines the two constraints without assuming that an exact minimum circuit supplies same-block raw pairs.

The November 25 public project update reports 87 additional six-input monotone cases where NOT gates save no gates, and lists the four-output shared-gate case as under investigation. There is no announced resolution of P versus NP. [Public status and update](https://data.pequalnp.com/head.json)

### Joint equality on two truth-table blocks

[MCSPJointEquality.lean](frontier/MCSPJointEquality.lean) splits the truth table on one represented variable. Each block has an independent prefix function and affine phase tag. One suffix selector works for both blocks. Exact MCSP at cutoff $m^2$ accepts a splice precisely when both tag pairs match, even after any four flips in the combined table. A mismatch in either block recovers the selector with the same auxiliary information as in the one-block proof.

On the schedule $s=2^r$, $m=r+s+2$, the joint cut requires $2(2^r+s+1)$ distinct crossing source wires. The count includes raw inputs. Placing every gate on one side can leave only raw-input crossings, so this count does not supply distinct gate charges outside the forest. The original global affine control rejects the gated-parity diagonal. A sound sparse control that runs the existing recognizer separately on the two fixed blocks and ANDs their answers passes the whole joint family with $o(N\log N)$ gates, where $N=2^m$.

### One control for every block coordinate

[MCSPJointOrbit.lean](frontier/MCSPJointOrbit.lean) transports the same joint equality restriction to all $m$ placements by swapping the block variable with each represented coordinate. Every transported cut retains its crossing-wire lower bound, including after four flips. One control passes every placement for every selector valid for the original joint test.

The diagonals have Boolean degree at most $r+1$. [MCSPDegreeSampler.lean](frontier/MCSPDegreeSampler.lean) reconstructs a degree-$d$ table from low-weight samples using at most $8dN$ gates. Five disjoint translates guarantee a clean sample when at most four entries are flipped. The control checks candidate block coordinates on the small sampling set and compares the full reconstructed table to the input once per translate. Acceptance implies exact MCSP acceptance. On $m=r+2^r+2$ the circuit uses at most $160(r+1)N=O(Nlog m)$ gates. Thus the simultaneous requirements for these transported cuts are compatible with $o(Nlog N)$ size.

### Long AND probes beyond the degree sampler

[MCSPLongAndOrbit.lean](frontier/MCSPLongAndOrbit.lean) adds a common signed conjunction on $m-5$ variables by XORing it into each joint splice. The probe costs at most $2(m-5)+1$ gates and has 32 ones. The positive conjunction vanishes on the unshifted low-weight sample while having degree above $r+1$. The four-flip control rejects every twisted diagonal, including after any four additional flips. A low-degree difference supported on at most 40 entries is zero, since one of 41 disjoint translated samples misses that support. This rules out explaining the AND twist as a different low-degree diagonal with four corrections.

A single selector works at radius 36. The widened control uses 37 translated samples and accepts only exact YES instances. It passes every block placement and every signed conjunction with five free variables, even when the AND mask and the four additional flips overlap. The proof also covers any common twist supported on at most 32 entries. The gate bound remains $O(Nlog m)$, so the widened family still permits a circuit below $Nlog N$.

### Signed long AND probes with growing support

[MCSPGrowingAndOrbit.lean](frontier/MCSPGrowingAndOrbit.lean) leaves $r-1$ represented variables free on $m=r+2^r+2$. An AND twist flips $2^{r-1}$ entries, between $m/4$ and $m/2$. One selector works for every block placement, choice of fixed variables and literal signs, and up to four further flips. The radius-36 control rejects all diagonals with this growing twist.

Six separated sampling sets suffice to recover the low-degree part. A face can intersect at most one of the sets and the four noise flips hit at most four others. [MCSPFaceDecoder.lean](frontier/MCSPFaceDecoder.lean) then shares support counts across address halves. A fixed coordinate has at most four residual ones with the wrong value, while both values of a free coordinate have more than four. This recovers the face in $O(N)$ gates and leaves one control of size $O(Nlog m)$ for all placements. Its acceptance implies exact MCSP acceptance, so these tests are compatible with $o(Nlog N)$ size even when the AND support grows.

### Multiplicity of original downstream gate charges

[MCSPChargeMultiplicity.lean](frontier/MCSPChargeMultiplicity.lean) adapts Cartography's physical-read count to pairs of block tags and original gate indices. Counting through the two input ports bounds the existing first-exit footprint by four tags and the second-exit footprint by eight. The earlier estimates were six and eighteen. For distinct blocks of a fixed partition, the sum of locally witnessed charges is at most four or eight times the number of original indices in their union. The eight-tag bound also supplies the footprint premise of the existing greedy selector. [Cartography’s incidence argument](https://github.com/yo4e/PvsNP-Cartography/blob/ba1da266a3e51f4025b7ba4f00d305b94796cbff/expeditions/043-dispersed-shifts/physical-cost.md)

For minimum exact MCSP at arity 32, the individually repaired 256-entry block restrictions certify at least **2,079,857 distinct original first-exit gates** across the eligible prefixes. These are witnesses from separate restrictions. Applying the whole count in the forest inequality would require a joint restriction that preserves the residual complexity bound.

The signed-AND control admits an equivalent implementation with an explicit all-zero branch. An aggregate OR feeds one negation, which reads a constant under every nonempty all-one pin set of at most half the table. The restricted output stays nonconstant, and the circuit still has $O(Nlog m)$ size. This exhibits high multiplicity for the general downstream-reader predicate in an implementation without a size-minimality premise. The local bounds use the certified predecessor footprints.

## Complementary routes

1. **Direct general-circuit lower bounds.** Find a single explicit language in NP whose circuits exceed every polynomial size bound. The frontier checkout already contains parity gate elimination and the $3.5n-7d$ affine-disperser bound for the full binary basis. These give named baselines for validating a new restriction argument. The parity-addressed multiplexer used in Andreev's function is a useful control because its parity blocks and multiplexer give linear-size general circuits while restricted threshold-circuit models exhibit substantial lower bounds. [Pinned gate-elimination code](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/GateElimination.lean) · [Pinned affine-disperser code](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/AffineDisperser.lean) · [Andreev threshold bounds](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/330/)
2. **Uniform proof search.** Resolution automatability asks for a refutation in time polynomial in the input length and the length of a shortest refutation. Atserias–Müller give a reduction from 3-SAT to this problem. Under $P=NP$, the existence of a refutation of bounded length can be decided in polynomial time in the instance and the unary length bound, then a refutation can be recovered bit by bit. This makes an unconditional uniform proof-search lower bound a direct separation target. The existing Lean resolution and Tseitin results give test cases for the proof-length premise. [Atserias–Müller](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02991) · [Pinned Lean resolution lower bound](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Logics/Propositional/Resolution/TseitinExpander.lean)
3. **Algorithmic route to $P=NP$.** The existing [Cook–Levin target](PvsNP/Problem.lean) takes a uniform polynomial-time decider for all valid encoded 3-CNFs. A proposed simplification or decomposition should be evaluated by its worst-case total number and size of residual instances in encoded input length. The October counter provides a subexponential endpoint for suitable residual instances. The polynomial-time target remains the criterion for this route. [Clay problem description](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/) · [October counter scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/lean/docs/378.md)

## What the October result establishes

The 1999 Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) says that the infimum of the achievable exponential rates for deterministic 3-SAT is positive, using the number of variables as the parameter. The OpenAI and Anthropic preprints give deterministic worst-case **exact model counting** at a subexponential rate for every fixed clause width. For a formula with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses, OpenAI's manuscript gives a polynomial factor in $n+m$ and exponent $O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})$, improving to $O_{k,D}(n/\sqrt{\log n})$ when $m\le Dn$. Its Lean scope uses the exponent $O_k(n(\log\log n)^2/\sqrt{\log n})$ with polynomial time overhead and space in encoded input length. Anthropic's `ethIsFalse` and `mainTheorem` use Mathlib's multistack machine model. Their statement file and Appendix E describe the machine, valid inputs and differences from the printed theorems. [Original ETH definition](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/ImpagliazzoPaturi_2001_jcss.pdf) · [OpenAI manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026) · [OpenAI Lean scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/lean/docs/378.md) · [Anthropic statement and replay](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/README.md)

The fixed-width result also sets every fixed-$k$ rate to zero, refuting the usual deterministic SETH, which quantifies over fixed widths. In particular, a single uniform improvement parameter can be chosen before $k$ while the machine and polynomial overhead may depend on $k$. The frontier library states both negations in one multitape model. Unbounded-width CNF-SAT is a separate runtime question because the constants and degrees may depend on the width. For P versus NP the target is polynomial in the input bit length $L$, which is a much stronger speedup. On sparse canonically encoded 3-CNFs, $L=O(n\log n)$ and therefore $L^c=2^{O(\log n)}$ for every fixed $c$. Each displayed SAT runtime exponent is much larger than $\log n$. A superpolynomial *upper bound* leaves the existence of a polynomial-time algorithm open. [Complexitylib formal statements](https://www.cslib.io/complexitylib/abstracts/subexponential-sat.pdf)

There is independent mathematical reconstruction. Ryan O'Donnell reformulates the problem as a sparse permanent and uses interpolation from derivatives at points with exploitable block structure. Pooya Hatami's November 18 notes develop that argument and connect it back to 3-SAT by sparsification and a size-controlled reduction. Anthropic's route instead improves tensor-network contraction by decomposing swap operators and regrouping the network along rows or columns. The community forum records Lean replays and ongoing scrutiny. [O'Donnell exposition](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/some_eth_false_exposition.pdf) · [Hatami notes](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~hatami/expositions/ETH.pdf) · [Anthropic method](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/README.md) · [Community review](https://ethdiscussion.blog/)

The OpenAI paper derives $E^{NP}\not\subseteq\mathrm{SIZE}(O(n))$ using Williams's witness-circuit argument and subexponential-time SAT for linear-size circuits. With the manuscript's sharper sparse bound it gives an $E^{NP}$ language requiring $\Omega(n\sqrt{\log n})$ gates at infinitely many input lengths. Here $E^{NP}$ is deterministic linear-exponential time with an NP oracle. A superpolynomial general circuit lower bound for an explicit language **in NP** would imply P ≠ NP via $P\subseteq P/\mathrm{poly}$. [OpenAI Appendix C.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex) · [Quantitative argument](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-circuits.tex) · [Williams's transfer theorem](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf)

## ETH follow-up experiments

### 1. Preserve the SAT speedup for larger circuits

The first checked control family is the expanded multiplexer. Factoring its repeated selector tests reduces $\Theta(n\log n)$ gates to $O(n)$ with exact model counting and an $o(n)$ exponent at the published interpolation scale. [Multiplexer experiment](README.md#checked-multiplexer-compression)

**Start here.** Use the verified circuit-to-CNF construction as a baseline. Put $V=n+s$ for its declared variables. With the formalized interpolation-scale counter, the exponent is bounded by $O(V(\log\log V)^2/\sqrt{\log V})$. This gives $2^{o(n)}$ for $s=o(n\sqrt{\log n}/(\log\log n)^2)$. The manuscript's sparse bound allows $s=o(n\sqrt{\log n})$. Both statements assume polynomial encoding and processing overhead. For a family retaining $s=\Theta(n^2)$ gate variables, the sparse formula gives an upper-bound exponent of order $n^2/\sqrt{\log n}$, so direct enumeration of the $n$ inputs gives a better guarantee. The frontier library already proves the linear-size case and exact counting with reachable-wire pruning, including the multiplier for unused inputs. [Circuit counting source](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/blob/6a5fb39b1da33ce1f41adf0f9b234982e48e33ef/Complexity/Computability/Complexity/FineGrained/CircuitCounting.lean) · [OpenAI manuscript and bounds](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026)

**First research milestone.** Pick a bounded-fan-in circuit family with substantial gate sharing and $s$ above the range of the baseline bound, for example $s=\Theta(n\log n)$. Try eliminating subcircuits with small input and output interfaces by replacing each with its exact Boolean relation. Also test whether Mishra–Santhanam's substitutions on independent parameters can be turned into an algorithm whose branches admit sparse CNF representations. For counting, prove a disjoint partition of the inputs and a unique completion in each branch, or prove the multiplicity correction explicitly. Charging $2^b$ branches gives an exponent budget of

$$
b + O\left(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\frac{(\log\log V_{\mathrm{eff}})^2}{\sqrt{\log V_{\mathrm{eff}}}}\right).
$$

For a positive result, exhibit a deterministic construction with fixed residual clause width, polynomial processing per branch, and the displayed budget $o(n)$ uniformly over the family. The bound $b=o(n)$ and $V_{\mathrm{eff}}=O(n(\log n)^{1/3})$ is one sufficient target. Compare with reachable-wire pruning and with direct dynamic programming on the same interfaces. Either a provable improvement over those baselines or a family forcing the budget over $n$ for a precisely defined compiler would resolve a concrete question. ### 2. Find exactly where sparse permanent interpolation loses its speedup

Follow O'Donnell's proof and formalize the first decomposition identity, then compare the bounded-degree case with matrices having high-degree rows or columns. An elementary baseline handles an $n\times n$ matrix with $h$ exceptional rows and columns in total. Branch on the partner of each exceptional vertex still present and delete the matched pair. Each perfect matching appears once and the residual degree is bounded. This adds at most $h\log_2 n$ to the exponent, leaving it $o(n)$ when $h=o(n/\log n)$. Try to improve that charge by handling several exceptional vertices together, with explicit bounds on the Taylor coefficients and their bit lengths. Include an adversarial family with the same sparsity budget but poor block structure. This track probes which parts of the 2026 speedup can be reused in other exact counting problems. [O'Donnell's construction](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/some_eth_false_exposition.pdf) · [Dell et al. on size-controlled permanent reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1775)

### 3. Use proof complexity as a complementary lower-bound track

Choose an explicit family of unsatisfiable formulas and a named proof system such as resolution. Formalize its encoding and a known size lower bound first, then test the same combinatorial invariant against a specified stronger system. A counterexample or simulation that destroys the invariant is a concrete outcome. Track verifier time, proof length and proof search time separately. The Cook–Reckhow characterization describes the stronger result needed to connect this route to P versus NP.
